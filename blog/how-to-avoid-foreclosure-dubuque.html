Facing foreclosure on your home is frightening and can leave you with more questions than answers. There’s many reasons why you may find yourself in this challenging situation, and it’s important to remember that you’re not alone. The good news is that you have options! To avoid foreclosure Dubuque residents need to face the situation head-on and not wait for it to get worse. By taking the right steps, you may be able to avoid the pain of foreclosure and find a solution that puts your mind at ease.

What is Foreclosure

Foreclosure happens when a lender repossesses a property after the homeowner doesn’t make the required mortgage payments. This legal process is allowed because the home acts as collateral for a loan and can be taken back to cover the outstanding balance due to non-payment. In Dubuque, the foreclosure process typically begins when the homeowner misses four consecutive monthly payments, or is around 120 days delinquent on their loan.

What to Do if You’re Facing Foreclosure in Dubuque

If you’re facing foreclosure in Dubuque, it’s important not to ignore the situation, since it becomes more challenging the longer you wait. Here are the steps you can take to try to avoid foreclosing on your home.

Contact Your Lender - If you’re unable to make payments on your mortgage, it’s important to reach out to your lender and speak to them about your situation. They can often work with you to find a solution that helps you avoid defaulting on your loan.

Seek Expert Advice - Dubuque residents have access to financial counselors who can provide free, expert advice. You can contact Iowa Mortgage Help or call a HUD-approved counselor to learn more.

Don’t Avoid Your Mail - It can be tempting to ignore notices from your lender, especially if you feel like there’s nothing you can do. However, many of these letters contain important information regarding help options and deadlines that you won’t want to miss.

Options to Avoid Foreclosure on Your Home

While facing foreclosure is scary and overwhelming, you do have choices. The earlier you take action, the more options you have and the better the final outcomes may be.

Modify Your Loan

When you speak to your lender, they may give you the option to change the terms of your mortgage so your payments are more affordable. Some refinancing agreements even allow you to skip one or two payments, giving you time to replenish your funds and get back on track.

Set Up a Forbearance Plan

Depending on the reason behind your missed payments, you may be able to temporarily stop or lower your payments. Temporary hardship cases are determined on an individual basis, so talk to your lender to see if you qualify.

Perform a Deed-in-Lieu of Foreclosure

Transferring ownership of your house to the lender can help you avoid foreclosure. However, with this option, you lose any equity you’ve put into the property and will not receive any funds once the process is complete.

Sell with a Realtor

To avoid the foreclosure process, you can sell your home with a realtor. Listing on the traditional market will help you get more money, but it can take time, with many homes in Dubuque taking 3 to 4 months or more to sell.

Sell for Cash

Selling your house for cash to a direct investor or buyer helps you avoid foreclosure or the drawn-out selling process with traditional market sales. It’s a fast process, generally taking only a few weeks, and you don’t have to worry about making repairs, cleaning, or going through annoying showings. Tips to Avoid Foreclosure in Dubuque

To help avoid foreclosure Dubuque residents can use these helpful tips.

Act Quickly - Once the foreclosure process starts, it’s out of your hands. Fortunately, you have around 120 days to take action and speak to your lender. Many times, they’re happy to work with you to create a solution to your unique situation.

Get Help - Facing foreclosure alone can feel isolating and overwhelming. You don’t have to go through it alone. There’s numerous free financial counselors available to Dubuque residents to give you guidance on your next steps.

Be Aware of Scammers - Scammers love to take advantage of vulnerable homeowners, often offering to save your home for a large fee. It’s best to speak to your lender directly and remember that if a deal seems too good to be true, it probably is.

Look at Alternatives - If you can’t keep your home, selling it can be a good alternative. While you may get more with a traditional sale, you may not have the time required before foreclosure starts.
